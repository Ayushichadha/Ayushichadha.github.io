The project grew over roughly a year of reading, studying, building, and deciding which questions I could turn into experiments.</p><h2>The questions that came first</h2><p>Andrej Karpathy’s vision of a cognitive core was an important influence: intelligence that can reason and draw on tools and knowledge. His posts and reading suggestions led me further into cognition, including Daniel Kahneman’s <em>Thinking, Fast and Slow</em> and David Eagleman and Jonathan Downar’s <em>Brain and Behavior</em>.</p><p>I was interested in how people learn from limited experience, how memory contributes to reasoning and imagination, and how dreams and creativity combine experiences in unfamiliar ways. These were motivations and questions, rather than claims that a particular model reproduces human cognition.</p><h2>Choosing something I could investigate</h2><p>HRM gave me a recurrent latent reasoner in which I could study internal computation directly. FeUdal Networks suggested a way to introduce directional goals, while the options framework helped me think about persistence and temporal abstraction. The question became concrete: how long should an internal subgoal guide computation before it is replaced?</p><p>My reading also included work on chain-of-thought prompting, adaptive computation, dual-process theories, the measurement of intelligence, and ConceptARC. I referred to these works at different depths as the project developed. They helped me choose a model, formulate hypotheses, and design experiments.</p><h2>Persistence, then revision</h2><p><em>When to Re-Plan</em> investigated persistence as a control variable in hierarchical latent reasoning. The next question was whether a controller could learn when to revise a commitment, and whether those decisions improved the final result.</p><p>That became <em>Beyond the Clock</em>. Its central distinction is between a score responding to state, a policy changing its decisions, and those changes adding outcome value. Reading about GEPA, AlphaEvolve, and Meta-Harness broadened my interest in applying this evaluation question to systems that supervise other processes. My experiments so far use an internal reasoner.</p><p>The conversation around Astra has been exciting to follow because of these interests. Reports about recurrent depth are a reason to investigate further, rather than evidence that its architecture matches my own work.</p><h2>Learning along the way</h2><p>In September 2025, I advanced to stage-two technical assessments for the Anthropic Fellows Program. In June 2026, I advanced through the MATS empirical assessments and was asked to complete additional written responses for Redwood. These were application stages, not fellowship appointments.</p><p>I plan to write more about the reading, experiments, and feedback that shaped this work on <a href="https://substack.com/@ayushi25">Substack</a>, as I work through my handwritten notes.</p><p><a href="/reading/">Selected reading and conversations</a> · <a href="/#research">Research papers</a></p></div></main><footer><span>Ayushi Chadha · India</span><a href="mailto:ayushichadha48@gmail.com">Get in touch</a></footer></div></body></html>
