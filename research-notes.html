Research notes

From persistence to decision value.

How long should a reasoner keep an internal goal—and what would make revising it worthwhile?

Starting with internal goals

My early reading focused on HRM, FeUdal Networks, and temporal abstraction through the options framework. These provided a starting point for investigating persistent directional subgoals inside recurrent reasoning.

When to Re-Plan studies persistence as an explicit variable. It was accepted at the CompLearn Workshop at ICML 2026. Earlier technical notes document the architectural background.

Asking whether adaptation helps

Beyond the Clock uses a compact hierarchical reasoner to distinguish state-dependent scores, changed decisions, and improved outcomes. Frozen-checkpoint interventions isolate timing from changes in the model’s learned parameters.

The tested learned policies did not outperform the best observed forced schedules on their respective checkpoints. These are diagnostic results in a small testbed: the strongest schedule was selected using final evaluation outcomes, exact puzzle accuracy was zero, and the exhaustive timing and oracle analyses covered one seed and a constrained schedule space. They do not establish a general limit on adaptive revision.

A broader reading direction

My reading expanded to GEPA, AlphaEvolve, and Meta-Harness: systems that revise prompts, programs, or executable harnesses. They brought the broader problem of assigning credit to supervisory decisions into focus. My experiments remain on an internal reasoner; the connection to these systems is methodological.

Questions for the next stage

I’m interested in how outcome-aligned credit changes revision policies, how to compare them against fixed schedules chosen on separate calibration data, and how decision budgets affect the available adaptive value. Extending the analysis across seeds and more capable systems would help establish where the evaluation method transfers.

These are research directions, rather than claims of completed experiments.
