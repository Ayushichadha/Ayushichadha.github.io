Ayushi Chadha

I work on reasoning and agentic systems, with a particular interest in latent reasoning in hierarchical recurrent models: how internal computation develops across recurrent depth, how medium-horizon intent can be represented in latent space, and when that intent should persist or be revised.

My recent work approaches these questions through hierarchical control and adaptive computation. My first paper studies persistent directional subgoals inside a recurrent latent reasoner. My second studies learned supervisory control and asks whether an apparently adaptive internal signal actually produces useful decisions.

Before this work, I spent some time in machine learning and applied AI at Propero, where I led the development of ShopiBot, a domain-specific AI system, and worked across retrieval, agentic workflows, evaluation, and ML system design.

I am interested in research and engineering work around reasoning, adaptive computation, agents, and model-facing systems.

Selected Research

When to Re-Plan: Subgoal Persistence in Hierarchical Latent Reasoning

Accepted paperSole author

2nd Workshop on Compositional Learning: Safety, Interpretability, and Agents at ICML 2026

Seoul, South Korea · July 11, 2026 · Listed in Poster Session 1 on the official ICML schedule · Organized by researchers from IBM Research, OpenAI, Harvard, ETH Zürich, the University of Amsterdam, and Zhejiang University.

This paper asks how long a hierarchical latent reasoner should preserve an intent before revising it, extending the Hierarchical Reasoning Model with a manager-worker interface that emits persistent directional subgoals in latent space.

It finds that subgoals must persist long enough for longer-horizon structure to form while remaining revisable, so more frequent re-planning is not necessarily more adaptive.

A Score Is Not a Policy: Measuring Whether a Supervisory Module's Decisions Are Worth Making

Preprint · Under review · Sole author · 2026

This paper asks whether a learned supervisor can use a reasoner's internal state to decide when an active commitment should persist or be revised.

It separates state dependence, behavioral adaptation, and decision value, finding that an apparently adaptive score need not produce useful interventions while strong fixed policies can capture much of the available value.

PaperCode to be released

Together, these projects study a broader problem in reasoning systems: how computation should be structured over time, when internal intent should change, and how to tell whether learned control over those decisions is genuinely useful.
